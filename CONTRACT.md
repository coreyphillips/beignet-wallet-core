# Shared wallet contract

Import from `@beignet/wallet-core` (file dependency on `../shared` for web, `../../shared` from beignet-rn/beignetrn). Pure ES modules plus TypeScript declarations; no browser-only globals at import time.

`new WalletClient({ url, token, walletId? }, { fetch?, now?, store? })` or `new DemoWalletClient()` implements the same methods. `store` is an optional `{ load(walletId), save(walletId, rows) }` that keeps each wallet's completed activity between launches; without it the ledger below lives in memory for the client's lifetime. Malformed stored rows are ignored and a store that fails never fails a read.

- `listWallets(): Promise<WalletRecord[]>`
- `createWallet({name, network: 'mainnet'|'regtest'|'testnet', primaryUri?,electrum?:{host,port,tls},mnemonic?,recoveryAutoApply?}): Promise<WalletRecord & {mnemonic?:string,warnings?:string[]}>`: explicit mutation; creates external LFBW, default primary only on mainnet. Creation notices are limited to five nonempty strings of up to 512 characters and do not enter snapshots. `mnemonic` (12 to 24 words) restores instead of generating. `recoveryAutoApply: true` is the explicit embedded-only opt-in to import peer-storage channel backups for that phrase: it requires the phrase, `getConfig().recoveryAutoApplyAvailable`, and that the previous wallet is closed; the runtime then holds every mutating operation until the recovered channel state is installed (`getRecoveryStatus().importPending`). Refused with `RECOVERY_UNAVAILABLE` where unsupported and `DEMO_ONLY` on the demo client.
- `selectWallet(id): void`: sets current wallet. `connection` is readable.
- `snapshot(): Promise<WalletSnapshot>`: `{wallet, balance:{totalSats, availableSats, pendingSats, pending:{unconfirmedSats, confirmedSats, openingSats, splicingSats, closingSats, erroredSats}, receivableSats, offlineReceivableSats?}, activity:Activity[], primary:{uri,connected,setup,setupError?}, primaries?:PrimaryStatus[], primariesThresholdSat?, primariesReceiveTarget?, notes:string[], updatedAt:number, demo:boolean}`. `primary` is the rank-1 primary (what `updatePrimary` edits); `primaries`, present when the engine ranks several (the wallet record lists `lfbw.primaries`), is the whole ranked list with each primary's connection, setup, balance, channel count and place in the receive order, and `primariesReceiveTarget` names the one a receive that names no peer goes to. Timestamp numbers are milliseconds. `activity` is this read's rows folded into the wallet's ledger of completed rows: a completed row never disappears and never moves back to pending, expired or failed when a later read, lookup or relaunch shows less; a read that is itself complete for the same id replaces the remembered row (a fee becoming known, a receipt reclassified as a transfer). `pending` (always present in a snapshot, optional in the type so hosts' fixtures still type-check) is what `pendingSats` is made of and always sums to it: unconfirmed deposits, confirmed on-chain funds not yet in a channel, a channel still opening, a splice still locking, a channel's balance coming back from a close, and funds needing recovery attention. `closingSats` is the engine's pending-close figure; it drops to 0 once the engine sees the sweep of a closed channel's balance in the wallet (beignet #1065). `offlineReceivableSats` is the most an offline receive can take right now, from the device engine's `GET /receive/offline`: 0 when no channel can hold one (a funded channel qualifies when a concurrent profile is negotiated; an existing immutable book prevents a second book on that channel). It is absent when the engine does not report it, as on a host's daemon.
- `prepareSend({request, amountSats?, max?, feeRate?}): Promise<SendReview>`: `{id, destination, description, amountSats, feeSats, feeLabel, estimatedFeeSats?, totalSats, route:'lightning'|'bitcoin', method?:'direct-funding', feeRate?, feeRates?, expiresAt:number, warnings:string[]}`. Quote only; will not send. For Lightning, `feeSats` is the maximum routing fee the payment is held to (`feeLabel` "Maximum routing fee"): the engine's estimate plus `LIGHTNING_FEE_HEADROOM_SATS` (10), because the engine floors its estimate to whole sats while enforcing the cap in millisats, and a retry may need a slightly costlier route. `estimatedFeeSats` is the priced route's fee, usually what is paid; `totalSats` is `amountSats + feeSats`, the most the payment can cost. Explicit amount mismatch refused. A Bitcoin request carrying a valid, unexpired `bgnq` direct-funding envelope is reviewed with `method:'direct-funding'` when `GET /utxos` holds a confirmed coin covering the amount plus the 1,000 sat fee ceiling (`feeLabel` "Maximum network fee"); otherwise the ordinary splice-out is quoted, with a warning saying why when an envelope was present. A unified request that also carries an invoice stays Lightning first: the invoice is the payment when `GET /liquidity`.sendableSats covers the amount, and the direct funding is the payment when it does not and such a coin exists. `feeRate` is whole sats per virtual byte, 1 to `MAX_FEE_RATE` (400, the engine's splice ceiling), refused with `INVALID_FEE_RATE` otherwise on every route; it prices only a payment to a Bitcoin address by splice and is ignored on the Lightning, direct-funding and drain routes. A Bitcoin address is paid out of one channel (the usable one with the primary; with ranked primaries the usable one holding the most, else one at a peer no longer ranked) as before. When that channel cannot cover the amount and `getConfig().channelSendAvailable`, the engine is asked for its channel-send plan (coreyphillips/beignet#1922): a `single` plan pays from the channel it names, a `composite` plan pays out of several channels in one transaction and the review carries `channelSend:{kind:'composite', channels}`; the engine's refusal leaves the ordinary `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. A max payment stays a one-channel splice. Lightning sends refuse `PRIMARY_DOWN` only when no ranked primary is connected. A splice review reports `feeRate`, the rate it was priced at (the one given, else the engine's normal, never above 400), and `feeRates`, the engine's `{fast, normal, slow, minimum}` estimates at quote time, each taking normal's figure where the engine left it out. The splice feerate is the rate times 250 sat/kw, floored at 253. `quoteMax({request, feeRate?})` takes the same rate, so a max chip matches the review it leads to.
- `send(review): Promise<SendResult>`: `{id,status:'completed'|'pending'|'uncertain'|'failed',amountSats,feeSats,txid?,paymentHash?,channelSend?,message}`. A composite review executes `POST /channel-send/execute` once: the engine's stage `LOCKED` is completed, `ABORTED` and `CONFLICTED` failed with its reason, any other stage pending, and the result carries `channelSend:{kind, channels, id}` (the engine's durable record id). Snapshots then list the engine's composite sends (`GET /channel-send/list`) as one sent row each, `channel-send:<id>`, in place of the transaction the merge would otherwise read as a channel funding transfer. Review is bound to this client/wallet, immutable internally, expires after <=60 sec, single use. An ambiguous network response returns `uncertain`; never retries or changes payment route. A direct-funding review posts `POST /direct-funding/send` once, under a lock keyed on the envelope: `CONFIRMED` is completed, `SIGNED_PENDING` and `MEMPOOL_SEEN` are pending, `FAILED` and `ABORTED` are failed with the engine's caveat. A throw (in-process, always pre-witness) or a `CREATED`/`OFFERED` status is a failure that spent nothing; the envelope is remembered as declined so the next `prepareSend` of the same request quotes the address payment. Over a host connection only a coded refusal is certain; a lost connection stays `uncertain` and the envelope stays locked. A max splice-out is priced again as it is sent: at the given rate when one was given, and at the engine's current normal otherwise, never dearer than reviewed.
- `quoteReceive({amountSats?, description?, mode?, peer?}): Promise<ReceiveQuote>`: `{id,amountSats,description,feeSats,netSats,expiresAt,warnings:string[],peer?,skipped?}`; amount may be omitted. With ranked primaries and no `peer` named, a JIT or offline quote is steered by the engine (coreyphillips/beignet#1922): the quote names no peer and the engine tries the primaries in its selection order; the answer's `peer` is the one that took it and `skipped` lists the ones passed over with the reason each (also said once in `warnings`); `NO_PRIMARY_AVAILABLE` is the engine's refusal when none takes it, and `PRIMARY_DOWN` here means no primary is connected. `peer` names one primary to quote at instead, as a resumed offline request does; the one-primary wallet is quoted exactly as before. `mode` is `'unified'` (the default when omitted: a plain invoice when the wallet's live channels with the current or the previous primary can take the amount, or a JIT invoice quoted through the primary when it has to provide the capacity, with Bitcoin and direct funding alongside) or `'offline'` (an explicit opt-in: requires `getConfig().offlineReceiveAvailable`, a fixed amount, a primary that offers offline settlement, and a channel that can hold the amount; refused with `RECEIVE_UNAVAILABLE` otherwise, before the review, and never downgraded to unified). Any other value is `INVALID_PARAMS`.
- `receive(quote): Promise<ReceiveRequest>`: `{id,uri,address,bolt11,paymentHash,amountSats,description,feeSats,expiresAt,warnings:string[],demo:boolean,offlineReceive?:boolean,peer?}`. A JIT or offline request is created at the primary its quote was priced at (`peer`), so quote and create agree on the peer and its fee. Unified BIP21 (Lightning + address + bgnq when supported). `offlineReceive` is true only for a request created from an `'offline'` quote, whose invoice stays payable while the wallet is closed; the quote's mode is bound to the review, so a quote cannot be spent under another mode (`REVIEW_CHANGED`).
- `getReceiveStatus(request): Promise<ReceiveStatus>`: read-only tracking for that Lightning hash or Bitcoin address: `{phase:'waiting'|'partial'|'pending'|'completed',receivedSats,confirmedSats,pendingSats,method?:'lightning'|'bitcoin',activityId?,paymentHash?,txid?,txids:string[]}`. An issued or pending invoice is not receipt evidence. Settled Lightning proves fulfillment even when deducted fees reduce its actual amount. Bitcoin uses exact matching script outputs from `GET /receive/onchain?address=`, never wallet-net transaction value, amount/date heuristics, or matching inputs. Below the requested amount is partial; reaching it unconfirmed is pending; the requested amount must be confirmed for completed. Amountless requests complete when all observed value is confirmed. `receivedSats` includes unconfirmed value; it does not imply spendable balance. Matching IDs are deduplicated and returned for optional Activity navigation; an Activity row may arrive later. Read failures reject, so the UI must keep its last observation and show the tracking error. Direct-funding receipt tracking is not included in this method.
- `importReceiveRequest(uri, expectedPaymentHash?): Promise<ReceiveRequest>` explicitly links a known original BIP21 to its wallet-issued invoice. The expected hash is checked before writing. The backend independently validates address ownership and immutable address/invoice/URI binding. This is for exact original requests only; amounts, timestamps, neighboring derivation indices, and generic Bitcoin receipts cannot recover an unknown association. Demo refuses real imports.
- `updatePrimary(uri, fallbackUri?): Promise<WalletRecord>`: with ranked primaries it replaces rank 1 and keeps the rest of the list.
- `listPrimaries(): Promise<PrimariesPolicy>`, `addPrimary({uri, fallbackUri?, rank?})`, `removePrimary(pubkey)`, `reorderPrimaries(pubkeys)` (every primary once, highest rank first), `setPrimariesThreshold(sats|null)`: the ranked primaries policy the engine owns (coreyphillips/beignet#1924), `{thresholdSat, receiveTarget, primaries:PrimaryStatus[]}`. Each write answers the policy after it and discards held receive quotes and send reviews. They require `getConfig().primariesAvailable` (`PRIMARIES_UNAVAILABLE` otherwise); a blank, fractional, negative or unsafe threshold is `INVALID_THRESHOLD` and a malformed pubkey or rank `INVALID_PARAMS`, before any request. A stopped engine lists the record's mirror with nothing connected. Removing a primary leaves its channels and funds where they are: they stay in the total, listed under `lfbw.retainedPeers`.
- `retrySetup(): Promise<void>`
- `getRecoveryStatus(): Promise<WalletRecoveryStatus>`: public recovery progress only, validated before it is returned: `{mode:'off'|'peer-storage'|'async-remote'|'quorum', state, importPending, importComplete, autoApply:{enabled,phase,lastReason}, capsuleCount, backupChannelCount, channels:[{channelId,status,restoreRecencyUnproven,fundingUnidentified}]}`. Never carries capsules, keys or transport credentials. The demo client answers `mode:'off'`.
- `diagnostics(): Promise<WalletDiagnostics>`: one read of what the engine reports about itself: the public `lfbw` record (including `lastChannelize` and `lastOffer`), block height, Electrum and primary connection, balances, `sendableSats`, coins with heights, channels with state, `htlcUsable`, `fundingConfirmed`, funding txid and splice figures, and the direct-funding policy. With ranked primaries, `primaries[]` gives each one's connection, transport and RTT, `channels[].withPrimary` is true for any ranked primary and `peerRank` names the channel's peer's rank. Each read that fails leaves its field `null`. Demo returns `{checkedAt, demo:true}`.

WalletRecord: `{id,name,network,status,lfbw?:{enabled,primaryUri?,primaryPubkey?,setup?,setupError?,primaries?,primariesThresholdSat?,receiveTarget?,retainedPeers?,unpairedFunding?,lastSplice?,lastChannelize?,lastOffer?,...}}`. `primaries` is the ranked list the record mirrors (pubkey, uri, fallbackUri, trusted, setup, setupError, connected, the offline-receive pair), present only on an engine that ranks several; the scalar fields are then rank 1's, and `setup` is ready when any primary is. `lastChannelize` is the wallet's last channelize decision, sanitized to `{action:'wait'|'splice-in'|'open'|'open-v2'|'failed', at, reason?, error?, code?, feeSats?, amountSats?, retryAt?, fallbackFrom?}`; `lastOffer` is the last direct-funding offer the wallet answered, `{state:'accepted'|'declined'|'failed'|'completed', reason, at}`. Snapshot notes derive the line about confirmed on-chain funds from `lastChannelize`, so a refused or waiting move is never described as progress.
`lfbw.setupError` and `snapshot.primary.setupError` are optional safe, actionable diagnostic strings. Known connection failures retain their cause and recovery action; arbitrary raw engine messages, secrets, and stack traces are never copied. Use the record returned by `listWallets()` to show setup errors when the stopped engine cannot supply a snapshot; do not invent balances as a fallback.
Activity: `{id,kind:'sent'|'received'|'request'|'transfer',title,description,amountSats,feeSats,status:'completed'|'pending'|'uncertain'|'failed'|'expired',timestamp,reference,txid?,paymentHash?,address?,dropped?}`. No secrets or payment preimages exposed. A `transaction:` row the engine reports with `dropped: true` (an unconfirmed transaction its server no longer has in the mempool or a block: replaced, a conflicting spend of its inputs confirmed, or the mempool evicted it) is `failed` with `dropped: true`, since it will not confirm. A later read that reports it without the flag shows it pending again, a confirmation outranks the flag, and a completed row the ledger remembers stays completed. A dropped funding transaction does not count toward `openingSats`. Ids are stable (`payment:<hash>`, `transaction:<txid>`, `submission:<id>`, the host's id for journal rows); a remembered row the read later reports under another id for the same reference, txid or payment hash is shown once.

New receive requests are durably registered with `POST /receive/requests {request}` before the QR is exposed. The backend returns `{request}` with its original creation time; `GET /receive/requests` returns `{requests}` across restarts. A failed registration throws `REQUEST_SAVE_FAILED` and never creates another invoice automatically. The original invoice may still exist in Activity.

Registered metadata includes `bitcoinTracking:'unique'|'ambiguous'`. Hosts that reuse an unpaid address can save every Lightning request, but all requests sharing that address are marked ambiguous. Shared never assigns Bitcoin payments to one of those invoices; exact Lightning hashes remain valid. Foreground Bitcoin tracking rechecks the current registry so another client's new registration cannot silently reuse an old uniqueness flag. It throws `AMBIGUOUS_RECEIVE_ADDRESS` with actionable text when needed. Snapshot retains each original QR and marks `receiveStatusUnavailable`, leaving the Bitcoin transaction independently visible.

If, and only if, `/address/new` refuses with the typed `RECEIVE_ADDRESS_LIMIT`, receive creation continues as an explicit `bitcoinTracking:'lightning-only'` request. Its `address` is absent, its `uri` is the actual BOLT11 invoice, and a warning explains that Bitcoin receiving returns after an existing Bitcoin receive address is used. JIT fee review and authorization, invoice validation, and durable registration still apply. No direct-funding request or Bitcoin receipt lookup is attempted. Other address errors still stop creation; the seed-recovery gap is never enlarged. This explicit mode is separate from legacy invoices whose original Bitcoin association is unknown.

Activity can include `receiveRequest` (the original public code, amount, description, expiry, optional creation time), `receiveStatus`, and `receiveStatusUnavailable`. Legacy invoice metadata has `legacy:true`, its exact `bolt11` as `uri`, and no guessed Bitcoin address. A stored request keeps the stable `payment:<hash>` Activity id when paid by Bitcoin. Exact linked output amounts replace duplicate generic received transaction value; other batch output value remains visible. Sent and internal transfer rows are never removed. Bitcoin status includes optional per-transaction `transactions:[{txid,amountSats,confirmed}]` for this accounting. Registration and request lookup failures leave balances usable with a status notice, never an invented completed payment.

Snapshots rotate at most two exact address lookups concurrently; observations cache for 30 seconds, completed observations for five minutes, and failed lookups retry after a minute. Foreground `getReceiveStatus` reads stay fresh and update that cache. A request settled over Lightning is final: it is never rechecked against its address, `getReceiveStatus` answers completed for it from the ledger even when the engine no longer lists the payment, and a later coin on that address is its own `transaction:` row rather than a partial payment. Expired registered requests remain eligible because Bitcoin payments can arrive after Lightning invoice expiry. A failed refresh retains the last known status and marks it unavailable. A Bitcoin transaction is an internal transfer when a channel from `GET /channels` names it as its `fundingTxid`, its `pendingSpliceTxid` (a splice still locking) or one of its `previousFundingTxids` (fundings a splice has replaced), unless it is an explicit submitted send. The last two need an engine that reports them (beignet-portable-engine #16); without them a splice-in reads as sent until it locks, and the funding it replaced reads as sent from then on. Confirmed funding transfers stay pending in Activity until their matching channel leaves actual funding/readiness states or their splice locks; disconnected `NORMAL` channels do not regress completed history. A disconnect, or a restart, puts a channel in `AWAITING_REESTABLISH` whatever state it was in. Core judges such a channel by its `preReestablishState` where the engine reports it (beignet #1899), and as locked when it has a `pendingSpliceTxid` or `previousFundingTxids`; otherwise it cannot tell, so a funding transfer reads as before and a direct-funding payment stays pending until the peer is back. A disconnected opening the engine names counts in the opening balance. Before any of that, a channel's `lockedFundingTxids` (beignet 0.36.0+) answers in any state, a closed channel's included: a funding it lists has locked, and one it does not list is judged as above. A closed channel stays in the list with what it locked, so a direct-funding payment into it stays completed. The receipt's own `lockedAt` is not used: a channel leaves the list only when the engine voids or abandons it, and a voided zero-conf open keeps a `lockedAt` for money that never arrived. A lock is the channel_ready or splice_locked exchange, so on a zero-conf channel it comes before the funding confirms.

When an exact lookup is due, it can delay the snapshot until that bounded read finishes (the engine receipt helper has a 12-second deadline; the host client's read deadline is 15 seconds). Failures are isolated per request and never discard the wallet balances or mark other successfully checked requests unavailable.

Exports `DEFAULT_PRIMARY_URI`, `DEFAULT_HOST_URL`, `parsePayment(input,{network?})`, `parseSats(input):number` (throws for invalid), `formatSats(number):string`, `WalletError` (code/status fields). Core never persists bearer tokens. UI must clearly label demo and keep demo selection explicit. A returned pending/uncertain result is not completed. Read failures must not be rendered as a zero balance.

`getConfig(): Promise<HostConfig>` reads `/api/config` (defaultElectrum, electrumPresets, supportedNetworks, capability flags: `offlineReceiveAvailable`, `jitQuoteAvailable`, `recoveryAvailable`, `recoveryAutoApplyAvailable`, `primariesAvailable`, `channelSendAvailable`, `engineVersion`). `primariesAvailable` gates the primaries editor; `channelSendAvailable` says an address can be paid out of several channels in one transaction (see `prepareSend`). An app talking to an engine without either flag shows the one-primary settings and the one-channel send exactly as before. Apps show the Receive offline opt-in only when `offlineReceiveAvailable` is true and the snapshot's `offlineReceivableSats` is absent or above 0, and read the flag through a promise so a client without the method leaves the control hidden. New wallets use peer-storage channel recovery. Creation unwraps the manager `{record,mnemonic}` response: display `mnemonic` only in the deliberate backup view, never serialize the returned object. `getRecoveryPhrase():Promise<string>` deliberately reads `/mnemonic`; callers must clear this secret when leaving backup UI. The core does not retain it.

`startWallet():Promise<void>` invokes the manager start endpoint on an explicit user selection/action. `refreshWallet():Promise<void>` invokes the safe daemon `/wallet/refresh` before manual refresh; ordinary snapshots never initiate it. Both are no-ops in demo.

The host adds `/api/wallets/:id/activity` for durable submitted Bitcoin payment records. Snapshot merges these records with daemon Activity and prevents duplicate rows after transaction verification. Address sends include `requestId`, `quotedFeeSats` and a bounded `description`; the host writes its journal before forwarding to the daemon. Unknown or pending initiations retain those statuses across app restarts until the host verifies the transaction.

`Activity` and `SendResult` expose optional `feeKnown` and `feeEstimated` flags. If `feeKnown === false`, show the fee as unavailable. If `feeEstimated === true`, the displayed amount is the reviewed estimate/cap, not a measured charge; use “Estimated fee” in Activity or “Reviewed fee” on the result. Missing daemon fees are never presented as a known zero. This includes received JIT payments whose daemon record does not expose its deducted fee.

## Embedded engine mode

`new EmbeddedWalletClient({runtime,walletId?,store?})` implements the same interface directly against an engine on this device (or a worker RPC for that engine). `runtime.request({method,path,body?})` returns the raw route result, not an HTTP `{ok,result}` envelope. Structured runtime errors preserve `{message,code,status?}`. `connection.url` is `embedded:`, token is empty, `embedded` is true, and demo is false. No fetch, remote wallet host or transport fallback exists in this class. All payment review, fee, expiry, single-use and activity rules remain in force.

The portable runtime owns the actual seed, synchronous durable database, wallet lifecycle, LFBW setup/channelize and durable activity journal. It must implement the same manager and daemon command shapes consumed by the existing client (including `/api/wallets/:id/activity` and `POST /direct-funding/send`, which is journaled before the engine is asked and reconciled from the engine's own payment record). The browser/native integration supplies network transports and secure durable storage to that runtime. The shared adapter itself is not an engine or a storage implementation.

`await embeddedClient.close()` clears unused reviews and asks `runtime.close()` to stop the engine. Repeated closes are harmless; further commands require constructing an unlocked runtime/client. The caller must await shutdown and flush durable storage before clearing its seed material.

## Concurrent offline receive

`balance.offlineReceivableSats` is capacity for a new request, not capacity already reserved. `balance.reservedInboundSats` and `unresolvedOfflineSlots`, when reported, describe existing reservations. They do not reduce `availableSats` a second time or move the home channel balance into pending. The engine's `htlcUsable`, spendable balance and channel balances remain authoritative. `offlineReservations` includes the channel, state, negotiated version and retained unknown slots. A DRAINING book can retain unknown reservations while ordinary payments use the rest of the channel.

`wallet.lfbw` and `primary` pass through optional `offlineReceiveAvailable` and `offlineReceiveReason`. Absence remains distinct from false or zero for older hosts.

An offline quote carries `mode: 'offline'` and its negotiated `concurrentVersion` when present. Retry `receive(quote)` after a transient failure, or refresh with `quoteReceive({mode: 'offline', amountSats, description, requestId: quote.id})` after expiry. The same ID is passed to the coordinator, which preserves its channel and selected profile across restart. A refreshed review never silently becomes an ordinary invoice. Proof sync does not create a completed Activity record; stored engine payment and invoice records remain the completion evidence.
